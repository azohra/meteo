One line per term, linking to the page that owns it. A few words here
deliberately carry two senses; those entries say so, because guessing the
wrong sense is worse than learning there are two.

### Document

A versioned JSON file the engine publishes at a stable path, accepted only
through a [contract guard](/docs/briefing/contract/). Profiles, manifests,
catalogues, smoke, observation, and site context are the document kinds.

### Profile

The per-site forecast document (`<model>/sites/<site>.json`) — the boundary
between generation and rendering. Older prose also says *site profile* and
*site-forecast document*: three names, [one document](/docs/briefing/profile-document/).

### Manifest

The small per-model discovery document beside the profiles: one publication's
identity, extent, and accounting. [Model manifest](/docs/briefing/manifest/).

### Model catalogue

`models.json`: which models exist and what each one declares — cadence,
levels, semantics, declared capabilities.
[Model catalogue](/docs/briefing/catalogue/).

### Site catalogue

`sites.json`: the launches you author — slug, name, coordinates, timezone,
identity only, nothing physical.
[Configure launches](/docs/forecast/configure-launches/).

### Site context

`site-context.json`: machine-measured ground truth per site — the elevation
pick, terrain, land cover. Not a catalogue: you author nothing in it.
[Site context document](/docs/briefing/site-context-document/).

### Launch and site

A **launch** is the place you fly; a **site** is that launch's record in
the data. The prose says *launch* about the world and *site* about the
files (`sites.json`, `site` block, `siteSlug`) — same referent throughout.

### Run

One model execution. A published run is identified by the pair
`(run.referenceTime, run.generatedAt)` —
[publication identity](/docs/compatibility/#publication-identity).

### Reference time

`run.referenceTime`: the model's initialization instant, the time every
forecast hour counts forward from. (The prose usually shows the identifier;
this is its English name.)

### schemaVersion and family

A document names its **family** with `schemaVersion`, and the package you
installed is the exact compatibility test.
[Compatibility](/docs/compatibility/) is the one rule's home.

### Capability

Two senses. Bare, it is a **product area**: the briefing, forecast,
station, grib, and j2k capabilities, as in `meteo <capability> <command>`.
Qualified — a **declared capability** — it is a flag in a document's
`capabilities` block: what a [model](/docs/forecast/model-capabilities/) or
a [station](/docs/station/what-your-hardware-shows/) states it can publish.

### Operator

The person or club running an engine deployment: they run the pipeline and
own the publication their audience sees.
[The responsibility boundary](/docs/#the-responsibility-boundary) lists
what is theirs.

### Engine and instance

The **engine** is the `@azohra/meteo.forecast` package; an **instance** is
one operator's deployment of it. The platform ships the engine only.
[Engine and CLI](/docs/forecast/).

### The read side and the write side

The **write side** is the engine: it derives and publishes documents. The
**read side** is `@azohra/meteo.briefing`: everything that is a pure
function of what was published. [The read side](/docs/briefing/).

### Meteogram

The chart the platform exists to produce: surface forcing aligned with the
atmosphere above one model grid cell.
[Reading a Meteogram](/docs/briefing/reading-a-meteogram/) explains every
mark.

### Strip

One of the lanes under the Meteogram's main panel — pressure,
precipitation, cloud, w\*, and the rest — sharing the time axis, each on
its own scale. [Reading a Meteogram](/docs/briefing/reading-a-meteogram/).

### Wind barb

The wind symbol: the shaft points where the wind comes **from**; a half
tick counts 5, a full tick 10, a pennant 50, in the chart's printed unit.
[Reading a Meteogram](/docs/briefing/reading-a-meteogram/).

### Boundary layer

The layer surface heating mixes. Its top is the
chart's dashed line, shortened to *BL top* in prose; *PBL* is the model's
own declared depth (`pblHeightM`, metres AGL) — the same question with
different physics, drawn beside it.
[Reading a Meteogram](/docs/briefing/reading-a-meteogram/).

### w\* (thermal velocity)

The convective velocity scale derived from surface heat flux and
boundary-layer depth — the w\* strip's number, stored as
`thermalVelocityMps`. An energy scale, not a predicted climb rate.
[Reading a Meteogram](/docs/briefing/reading-a-meteogram/).

### Usable lift

The modelled climb limit: where the strongest-core lift profile falls to
the sink threshold, unless cloud base stops it first — the chart's solid
line, stored as `usableLiftTopM`.
[Reading a Meteogram](/docs/briefing/reading-a-meteogram/).

### Sink rate

The glider-sink parameter usable lift is computed against: the engine
stores the 1.0 m/s answer, and the package recomputes the line for any
other. [Pure derivations](/docs/briefing/derive/).

### Scene

Serializable, renderer-independent geometry built from one validated
profile; the SVG serializer draws it, the hit-testing queries answer from
it. [Scene graph](/docs/briefing/scene/).

### Finding

A typed statement about one forecast — a `kind`, its numbers, the hours it
cites, and the thresholds it was computed with.
[Analyze a profile](/docs/briefing/analyze/).

### Stored and pure derivations

**Stored derivations** need raw model inputs, so the engine bakes them into
the document; **pure derivations** are functions of the published document
alone. [Who owns each value](/docs/#who-owns-each-value) is the split's home.

### History archive

The append-only monthly gzip JSONL of everything a dataset published, with
a sidecar byte-offset index beside each month.
[History archives](/docs/briefing/history-archives/).

### Ingest

The store-and-serve loop a consumer runs to hold published documents:
poll `runs.json`, ingest coherent publications, serve through gaps.
[Run an ingest](/docs/briefing/run-an-ingest/).

### Transport

Two senses. The briefing package's
[`/transport` subpath](/docs/briefing/transport/) loads published documents
consistently on the read side; the engine's
[provider transports](/docs/forecast/provider-transports/) are its
fetch strategies against ECCC and NOAA on the write side.

### Wire

Two senses, both from code. The station capability's
[**wire contract**](/docs/station/wire-contract/) is the live-station
document shape and its HTTP protocol. The engine's **`[wire]` report** is
the transport summary every build prints —
[Tune the wire](/docs/forecast/tune-the-wire/) reads it.

### Adapter

Two functions (`meta` and `load`) that turn one station vendor's upstream
into a wire document. [How adapters work](/docs/station/adapters/).

### Member

Three senses, each local to its page: an **ensemble member** (one of a
run's perturbations — [Ensemble values](/docs/briefing/ensemble-values/)),
a **comparison member** (one `(model, referenceTime)` pair —
[Compare profiles](/docs/briefing/compare/)), and a **gzip member** (one
independently decodable block of an archive —
[History](/docs/briefing/history/)).

### Feed

Two senses. A station **feed** is the `/feed` endpoint's document — every
configured station in one response
([Getting started](/docs/station/getting-started/)). A model **feed** is a
provider's published data stream
([Forecast model feeds](/docs/forecast/forecast-model-feeds/)).

### Absence is never zero

The platform's one data rule, everywhere: an optional field that is absent
means *not published* or *not measured* — a reader must never render it as
zero. [Compatibility](/docs/compatibility/) states it normatively.
